Greece: IVF, Open Donor Pool and the Only Legal Surrogacy in the EU

§ 01

In 2002, Greece passed Law 3089/2002 on medically assisted reproduction — one of the most progressive in Europe at the time. It permitted donor IVF for a wide range of people and — the headline news — legalised altruistic surrogacy under strict conditions. In the European Union, where surrogacy is prohibited or in a legal grey zone almost everywhere, this is a unique position. And it attracts patients from across the continent.

Greek IVF clinics have been accepting foreign patients for a long time and systematically. The combination of accessible legislation, a large donor pool, competitive prices and high professional standards is no accident. It is a deliberately constructed niche.

§ 02

Surrogacy in Greece: how it works

Greek Law 3089/2002 and its updates set strict conditions for surrogacy. The surrogate must be a Greek or EU citizen; she cannot be the genetic mother — donor or recipient eggs are used; the surrogate receives no payment, only expense reimbursement (food, medical costs, loss of income); the surrogate must be aged 25 to 50 and have children of her own; the agreement is approved by a court before the procedure begins — guaranteeing legal protection for all parties.

An important qualification: Greek law requires the intended parents to be a heterosexual couple (married or in a civil partnership) or a single woman. Same-sex male couples and single men do not have access to surrogacy under Greek law — a fundamental restriction worth understanding in advance.

⚖️ Key conditions for surrogacy in Greece

  • Intended parents must be a heterosexual couple (married or cohabiting)
  • The surrogate must be a Greek or EU resident
  • Medical necessity must be established (court approval required)
  • Altruistic only — no commercial surrogacy
§ 03

Donor IVF: why people choose Greece

Egg donation in Greece is one of the most developed markets in Europe. Key parameters: donor anonymity is guaranteed by law (unlike Denmark and the UK); the donor pool is very large — waiting times are considerably shorter than in Northern Europe (often 1–3 months versus 6–12 elsewhere); donors are primarily young women aged 18–35 who undergo extensive medical and genetic screening; clinics match donors by phenotypic characteristics — eye colour, hair, build, blood type.

§ 04

Who comes to Greece and why

Main patient categories: women with premature ovarian insufficiency or post-cancer treatment who need donor eggs; same-sex female couples needing both donor sperm and the procedure — Greece accepts such couples; single women seeking donor IVF; heterosexual couples needing surrogacy. For co-parents and those using co-parenting platforms: Greece can be a good option if one co-parent (female) needs donor eggs, or if both need a full donor cycle. Note that Greek law does not specifically regulate co-parenting — legal relationships are determined by the country of permanent residence.

§ 05

Legal warnings

Donor anonymity in Greece is Greek law. In the patient's country of residence, the situation may differ. A patient from Germany treated in Greece with an anonymous donor — the child may, under German law, have rights to donor information. Consultation with a lawyer in the country of permanent residence before treatment begins is essential. This is not a problem unique to Greece, but it requires a conscious approach.

Donor anonymity in Greece is protected by law. But in the patient's country of origin, the situation may differ. If you plan to return home to have your child recognised, verify how Greek birth certificates are treated in your jurisdiction before beginning treatment.

§ 06

The bottom line

Greece occupies a unique position on the European map of reproductive medicine: the only EU country with legal surrogacy and one of the leaders in donor IVF. For people who need donor eggs or a surrogate — and who meet the criteria of Greek legislation — this is a real and well-established path. For co-parents and single women considering donor IVF, Greece offers an optimal balance of donor availability, quality and cost.

Glossary

Glossary

Law 3089/2002

Law 3089/2002 — Greek law on medically assisted reproduction, which legalised donor IVF and altruistic surrogacy.

Altruistic surrogacy

Altruistic surrogacy — pregnancy carried without commercial reward, with expense reimbursement only. The only form of surrogacy legal in most Western democracies.

Donor pool

Donor pool — the database of registered egg or sperm donors at a clinic or in a country. Pool size determines waiting time and the diversity of choice.
